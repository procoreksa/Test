# English YouTube Channels on Scams, Fraud and Financial Crime: Competitive Benchmark (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: Direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl) were blocked by the sandbox network (DNS failure / proxy 403) during this session, so every figure below comes from **web-search snippets** of third-party trackers (Social Blade, vidIQ, CreatorDB, SpeakRJ, SponsorRadar, Socialcounts, OutlierKit) or press. All are estimates, not YouTube Studio data. The report writer should check them against the live channel pages before publishing. Anything I could not verify is listed under Gaps and is not given as a number.

## 1. Which channels lead, and their size (subscribers / views, with date and source)

### Takeaway
The niche splits into three clusters. (a) Investigative or explainer channels on financial crime: Coffeezilla (about 4.7M), ColdFusion (about 5.2M, general tech/business with strong fraud episodes), James Jani (about 2.2M), MagnatesMedia (about 1.86M), Patrick Boyle (about 1.3M) and Moon (about 1.3M, date unknown). (b) Scambaiting channels, which are the biggest by subscribers: Scammer Payback (about 9.3–9.5M), Kitboga (about 4M), Jim Browning (about 3.6M+, figure not dated) and Pleasant Green (about 1–1.5M). (c) Small guru-exposure channels such as Mike Winnet / Contrapreneurs (about 94–100K).

### Inferences
- James Jani has by far the highest views per video: about 94M across 28 videos, roughly **3.4M per video**. Coffeezilla gets about 1.1M per video, MagnatesMedia about 0.7M and Patrick Boyle about 0.38M. Jani shows that a few very polished long documentaries can match a high-volume channel. This is the most relevant model for a new faceless "smartest heists" channel.
- MagnatesMedia's growth has slowed (+10K/month), while Patrick Boyle is growing faster (+20–40K/month) with a low-production, high-cadence, commentary style. Coffeezilla grows fastest among the investigative channels because he breaks news on live scandals.
- Scambaiting channels have the biggest audiences, but their format (live calls, hacking) cannot be copied by a faceless narrated documentary channel. They count as adjacent competition for the same "scam" search demand, not as a template.

## 2. Format, video length, upload cadence, monetization signals

### Takeaway
Coffeezilla, Patrick Boyle and Mike Winnet are presenter-led. James Jani appears on camera in a cinematic essay style. MagnatesMedia, ColdFusion and Moon are narrated with B-roll and are faceless or near-faceless. Cadence ranges from James Jani's very rare uploads (28 videos in about 6 years) to Moon's 2 per week. Every channel shows sponsorship tracking on SponsorRadar.

### Cited Findings
- Coffeezilla: 539 videos since 2018, presenter-led investigative journalism (on camera in a set, with interviews such as three SBF interviews) — [SpeakRJ](https://www.speakrj.com/audit/report/Coffeezilla/youtube); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/pop-culture/who-is-coffeezilla)
- James Jani: 28 videos since Dec 2019 (very low cadence, long-form) — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/jamesjani/videos)
- Patrick Boyle: about 470 videos and still uploading in Sept 2026, so a high regular cadence — [Socialcounts](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-live-subscriber-count/UCASM0cgfkJxQ1ICmRilfHLw), [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@pboyle/)
- MagnatesMedia: about 255–295 videos since Dec 2018 — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@magnatesmedia/). A third-party course sells "How To Make Videos Just Like MagnatesMedia", which shows its faceless narrated format is seen as a template — [aggregator page (low quality)](https://admin.jesuits.africa/mediatakeoyr-he-stole-millions-from-investors-and-fled-the-country.html)
- Moon: 2 videos per week, faceless — [gist.ly](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/unveiling-faceless-youtube-channel-success-economics-explain-moon-reil)
- Monetization: SponsorRadar tracks sponsor deals for Coffeezilla, Scammer Payback, ColdFusion and Mike Winnet. ColdFusion's sponsor history runs to April 2026 — [SponsorRadar Coffeezilla](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/coffeezilla), [SponsorRadar ColdFusion](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/coldfusion)
- Scammer Payback is represented by the talent agency Night — [Night.co](https://night.co/talent/scammer-payback)
- Kitboga monetizes across YouTube and Twitch (1.3M followers) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitboga_(streamer))

### Inferences
- A faceless newcomer most resembles MagnatesMedia, ColdFusion and Moon (narrated documentary). James Jani's model of few uploads with very high production is the strongest benchmark for views per video.

### Gaps
- Typical video lengths per channel could not be verified from a source because the trackers could not be fetched. From general knowledge (unverified), Jani/MagnatesMedia/ColdFusion episodes often run about 20–60+ min and Patrick Boyle about 15–30 min, but these should be checked.
- No RPM/CPM or revenue figures were verified. NetworthSpot-style estimates are unreliable and were left out.

## 4. What makes them succeed; how they handle defamation/legal risk

### Takeaway
Success comes from three things: villain-driven narratives about real, often current, scandals; credibility (Boyle's finance background, Coffeezilla's direct interviews and documents); and production quality (Jani, ColdFusion). Legal risk is real. Logan Paul sued Coffeezilla for defamation in 2024, the case survived a motion to dismiss, and it was reportedly settled in July 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Logan Paul first threatened to sue in Jan 2023 ("See you in court"), then dropped the threat — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/118731/logan-paul-drops-threat-to-sue-coffeezilla-over-cryptozoo-criticism)
- He filed *Paul v. Findeisen* (No. 5:24-cv-00717, W.D. Texas) on June 27, 2024, seeking $75,000 plus fees. He alleged Findeisen repeated claims he knew were false and left out information showing Paul intended to build a legitimate game — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/07/08/logan-paul-lawsuit-crypto-zoo-coffeezilla/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/302492/logan-paul-sues-coffeezilla-for-defamation-over-cryptozoo-coverage)
- Findeisen called the suit an attempt to block his investigation of Liquid Marketplace, a Paul-linked company (Aug 2024) — [web3isgoinggreat](https://www.web3isgoinggreat.com/single/logan-paul-lawsuit-against-coffeezilla)
- In 2025 a magistrate judge recommended denying dismissal, finding the statements "reasonably capable of defamatory meaning" — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coffeezilla-should-not-duck-logan-paul-cryptozoo-claims-suit-judge); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/312505/logan-paul-cryptozoo-lawsuit-coffeezilla)
- A jury trial was set for May 4, 2026 but did not go ahead. A hearing was reset to June 29, 2026 — [Wrestling Attitude](https://www.wrestlingattitude.com/2026/06/logan-paul-cryptozoo-lawsuit-coffeezilla.html); [OpenClassActions](https://openclassactions.com/news/logan-paul-coffeezilla-defamation-lawsuit.php)
- A July 16, 2026 report says the case **settled out of court with undisclosed terms**, per CourtListener docket entries. This is single-source, so verify it — [CoinScoop](https://coinscoop.org/logan-paul-coffeezilla-cryptozoo-defamation-lawsuit-settled/). The sources conflict: a page current to Jul 10, 2026 still described the case as pending — [factually.co](https://factually.co/fact-checks/media/coffezilla-logan-paul-lawsuit-status-2026-301281)
- Legal standard: a public-figure plaintiff must show "actual malice" (knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for the truth) — attorney quoted by [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/118417/see-you-in-court-logan-paul-threatens-to-sue-youtuber-for-cryptozoo-scam-claims?amp=1)
- Jim Browning works under a pseudonym and admits his hacking methods are illegal under UK/US law. This is a different risk profile: criminal/computer-misuse risk rather than defamation — [Sophos](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/tech-support-scammers-hacked-back-by-vigilante); [thatsnonsense](https://www.thatsnonsense.com/?p=16759)
- Pleasant Green frames scambaiting as education. Viewers write that "you kept me from getting scammed" — [Yahoo News](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/nigeria-email-scam-ben-taylor-194153097.html)

### Inferences
- For a faceless channel, the safest topics are **adjudicated cases** (convictions, SEC/DOJ findings, court records: Madoff, Enron, Theranos, FTX/SBF, Wirecard). With those, statements can rest on public court records. Covering living people who have not been charged (influencers, current crypto projects) brings real litigation exposure, even for a well-resourced creator like Coffeezilla, whose case dragged on about 2 years. Wording such as "alleged", "according to the SEC complaint" and on-screen citation of documents reduces risk. This is general practice, not drawn from a specific source.
- A UK-based creator faces claimant-friendly UK libel law (no US-style actual-malice defence). This matters for MagnatesMedia/Jani-style channels. This is general legal knowledge, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No interview was found in which Coffeezilla, MagnatesMedia, Jani or Boyle describes their legal review or fact-checking workflow.
- The terms and final docket status of the Paul v. Findeisen settlement were not verified on CourtListener.
